      <div>
        <h2>Why High Ripple Current Matters</h2>
        <p>In power electronic circuits, aluminum electrolytic capacitors are subjected to AC ripple current components superimposed on the DC voltage. This ripple current generates internal heat due to the capacitor's Equivalent Series Resistance (ESR).</p>
        <p>Excessive internal heating accelerates electrolyte evaporation, leading to capacitance loss and eventual failure. <strong>High ripple current capacitors</strong> address this challenge through:</p>
        <ul style="color:var(--gray-600);margin-top:12px;">
          <li><strong>Low ESR design</strong> — Advanced anode foil etching and cathode foil optimization</li>
          <li><strong>Specialized electrolyte systems</strong> — Improved thermal conductivity and stability</li>
          <li><strong>Optimized internal construction</strong> — Multi-tab winding for reduced current density</li>
          <li><strong>Robust sealing</strong> — Extended operational life under thermal stress</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h2>Common Applications</h2>
        <ul style="color:var(--gray-600);margin-top:12px;">
          <li><strong>AC Frequency Inverters / VFDs</strong> — DC bus filtering and smoothing</li>
          <li><strong>Inverter Welding Machines</strong> — MIG, TIG, plasma, and stud welding power supplies</li>
          <li><strong>UPS Systems</strong> — Online double-conversion and line-interactive topologies</li>
          <li><strong>Solar & Wind Inverters</strong> — Grid-tie and off-grid inverter DC links</li>
          <li><strong>Servo Drives</strong> — Motor drive DC bus capacitance</li>
          <li><strong>Industrial Power Supplies</strong> — High-power SMPS and DC/DC converters</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
